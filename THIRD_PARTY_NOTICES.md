# Third-Party Notices

Trace the Law contains or references material that the project did not create.
The repository's MIT License covers project-owned code, schemas, documentation,
and metadata; it does not relicense third-party material.

This file is the human-readable notice index. Machine-readable per-source rights
metadata described in [RIGHTS.md](RIGHTS.md) controls whether a source artifact
may be captured, mirrored, transcribed, translated, summarised, or redistributed.
If this file and the per-source record disagree, publication must stop until a
rights maintainer resolves the conflict.

## Required notice entry

Add an entry before distributing a third-party artifact or substantial
third-party text:

```markdown
### <artifact or collection identifier>

- **Title:**
- **Publisher / rights holder:**
- **Original source:**
- **Source retrieved:** YYYY-MM-DD
- **Rights status:** open-license | public-domain | statutory-exception |
  permission | restricted | unknown
- **Licence or legal basis:**
- **Licence / terms URL:**
- **Attribution required:**
- **Permitted project uses:**
- **Excluded or embedded third-party material:**
- **Jurisdiction and material limitations:**
- **Last reviewed:** YYYY-MM-DD
```

Do not use an entry to make an unsupported public-domain claim. `Unknown` and
`restricted` materials may be referenced by public URL and bibliographic facts
but must not be mirrored or fully transcribed without further review.

Software dependency notices generated by package tooling may live with the
relevant code distribution, but any dependency copied into this repository
must retain its own licence and notices.

The absence of an item from this index is not evidence that the item is free of
copyright, database rights, privacy restrictions, attribution duties, or other
third-party rights.
