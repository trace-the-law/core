# Trace the Law Charter

## Mission

Trace the Law is a public, source-linked, versioned archive of material published
by governments, legislatures, regulators, courts, tribunals, and other public
authorities. It is built so people and software agents can inspect the evidence,
follow changes over time, and identify known gaps.

The project starts with one jurisdiction, but its rules are global. A country,
language, institution, or legal tradition must not be treated as the universal
default.

## The public promise

Trace the Law will:

1. Preserve provenance: who published an artifact, where it was found, when it
   was retrieved, and which exact version was processed.
2. Keep source material distinct from unofficial transcription, structured
   metadata, relationships, translations, and summaries.
3. Make corrections and supersession visible instead of silently replacing
   history.
4. Publish coverage boundaries and known gaps alongside the archive.
5. Apply source-specific rights, attribution, privacy, and publication rules.
6. Accept evidence-backed corrections through an open, reviewable process once
   its privacy-safe intake and authenticated receipts are launched.
7. Prefer the original language. Every translation must identify whether it was
   issued by an authority, prepared by a person, or generated by a machine.

These promises are subject to lawful removal, privacy protection, source safety,
and security response. Preservation is not a reason to republish material that
the project has no right to distribute or that would create disproportionate
harm.

## What Trace the Law is not

Trace the Law is independent and unofficial. It is not affiliated with,
endorsed by, or operated by a government, legislature, regulator, court, or
tribunal. Government names, seals, and logos must not be used as project
branding.

Trace the Law does not promise:

- a complete collection;
- an authoritative or official copy;
- a legally consolidated text;
- that a document is in force, applicable, or controlling;
- that no relevant document exists when a search returns no result; or
- legal advice or a conclusion about any person's situation.

Only the responsible authority can designate an official or authentic version.
An unofficial Markdown transcription remains unofficial even when it is exact.
A summary is a derived aid, not part of the source and not a substitute for it.

Coverage is always a dated claim about a declared jurisdiction, authority,
document kind, language, time range, and set of entrypoints. Its states are
`unknown`, `enumerating`, `covered_for_declared_scope`, `known_gap`, `stale`,
`degraded`, and `blocked`. `covered_for_declared_scope` never means that the
archive contains every relevant rule or authority.

## Scope

The archive may cover officially published constitutions, legislation,
regulations, rules, gazettes, orders, judgments, tribunal decisions, guidance,
notices, circulars, consultations, and announcements. Each artifact must retain
its type and claimed legal force; a press release or guidance document must not
be presented as binding law merely because it was published by an authority.

The default scope excludes leaks, unlawfully obtained material, private case
files, sealed material, and commercial publishers' headnotes or editorial
enhancements. A public contribution may identify an official source; it must
never contain the contributor's private legal facts.

## Evidence layers

Every reader and agent must be able to tell which layer it is using:

1. **Source manifest:** the publisher, entrypoints, enumeration method, and
   rights and access boundary declared for a source.
2. **Capture and artifact:** a capture preserves one source observation, URL,
   retrieval, and rights assessment; an artifact preserves only the immutable
   bytes identified by content hash. Identical bytes may have many captures.
3. **Derivation:** an append-only transformation record for unofficial OCR,
   Markdown, extraction, translation, or summary artifacts, naming inputs and
   methods.
4. **Record:** a stable identity for a public document and its issued or derived
   versions and provision snapshots.
5. **Relationship assertion:** a cited, reviewable project claim about how
   records relate, such as amendment, commencement, interpretation, or reversal.
6. **Evidence bundle:** a snapshot-bound selection for an agent, including cited
   dependencies, conflicts, unresolved items, coverage state, and checks still
   required.

No derived layer may overwrite or masquerade as a source artifact.

## Jurisdictions and expansion

Each jurisdiction adapter inherits this Charter and the global evidence model.
It must add a local source register, rights and privacy rules, institutional
hierarchy, language policy, coverage boundary, and named maintainers before
publishing data.

Countries are useful containers, not the only legal units. Supranational,
federal, subnational, municipal, treaty-based, Indigenous, and historical legal
systems must be representable without forcing them into an inaccurate country
hierarchy.

## Stewardship

Governance must remain public by default, evidence-led, and independent of the
identity of a contributor. Governments and commercial publishers may propose
corrections but receive no private overwrite or veto right. Sensitive privacy,
security, and legal-removal matters use private processes and the smallest
public record that is safe and lawful.
